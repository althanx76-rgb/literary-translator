# Korean as the target language (`ko`)

> Status: first draft. Native review welcome (see CONTRIBUTING.md).

Read this file when translating **into** Korean, together with the source-language file. Checker option: `--to ko`.

## Speech levels

- 합쇼체 (formal), 해요체 (polite), 반말 (familiar), 해라체 (narration and writing). Who speaks how to whom is the map of the forms of address, finer than a familiar/polite pair, and the switch from 존댓말 to 반말 is a narrative event, like the switch to the familiar form in European languages. Record it in the brief.
- Narration is normally in 해라체, past tense (-았다/-었다).
- **Second person.** Korean avoids an explicit "you": 당신 used as a generic "you" reads as translationese and sounds cold, hostile or marital depending on the context. Use names and titles (씨, 님), or leave it out.
- **Kinship terms as forms of address.** 형, 오빠, 언니, 누나 are very Korean: for foreign characters use them only if the scene supports them.

## Dialogue and punctuation

- “…” for dialogue, ‘…’ for thoughts.
- Western punctuation with Korean word spacing (띄어쓰기).
- The script flags non-Korean quotation marks (「」, 『』, « ») and Chinese or Japanese punctuation (。、).

## Names

Foreign names in Hangul follow the transcription rules of the National Institute of Korean Language (외래어 표기법). For Ukraine, since 2022 the Institute has indicated Ukrainian-based forms such as 키이우 and 하르키우 instead of 키예프 and 하리코프: agree the choice with the author. In the glossary, terms are also found when attached to particles.

## Swearing

Swearing (씨발, 개새끼, 젠장…) with the force of the original.

## Italics and emphasis

Italics are not part of Korean typography: emphasis uses 드러냄표 (emphasis dots) or ‘…’. The script doesn't compare italics.

# Ukrainian as the target language (`uk`)

> Status: first draft. Native review welcome (see CONTRIBUTING.md).

Read this file when translating **into** Ukrainian, together with the source-language file. Checker option: `--to uk` (`uk` is Ukrainian; British English is `en-gb`).

## When the story is set in Ukraine, Russia or the Soviet Union

The translation takes the characters back to their own language: it is a translation "back home", and the reader knows the story's world better than the author does.

- **Authentic realia.** What the original approximated goes back to its real name: institutions, ranks, offices, acronyms, official names, the bureaucratic and military jargon of the period, songs, food, brands. Check them against sources, not from memory. An error the original's reader doesn't see, a Ukrainian reader sees on the first page: flag it to the author in the notes.
- **Who speaks which language.** In the original everyone speaks the author's language. A character who in the story speaks Russian (or Surzhyk) can keep their lines in Russian, as Ukrainian fiction often does, or switch to Ukrainian: decide with the author, character by character, and record it in the brief.
- **Politically loaded choices.** Place names (Київ, Чорнобиль, Харків) and their Russian forms are positions, not details: propose them to the author and record them.

## Dialogue and punctuation

- Dialogue with a dash followed by a space: — Іди сюди, — сказав він.
- Quotation marks « », and „ “ inside them.
- One kind of apostrophe for the whole book (пʼять or п’ять).
- The script flags English or straight quotation marks, a dialogue dash without the following space, a hyphen or en dash instead of the dash, and different apostrophes inside Ukrainian words.

## Forms of address

- ти / ви. Respect also goes through first name and patronymic (Іван Петрович), intimacy through diminutives.
- **Vocative.** Someone addressing another person uses the vocative (Іване!, діду!, пане Тарасе!, Іване Петровичу!): leaving it out is a Russianism.

## Swearing

Choose the swearing according to the language the character speaks in the story (Ukrainian, Russian or Surzhyk), with the force of the original.

## Names and transliteration

Foreign names follow Ukrainian transcription conventions (Italian Giovanni → Джованні) and are recorded in the glossary. In the glossary, list the stems that change with the case (Київ*; Києв*).

## Italics and emphasis

Italics are part of Ukrainian typography: the script compares them with the original.

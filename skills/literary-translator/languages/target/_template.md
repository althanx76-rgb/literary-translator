# <Language> as the target language (`<code>`)

> Status: first draft. Native review welcome (see CONTRIBUTING.md).

Read this file when translating **into** <Language>, together with the source-language file. Checker option: `--to <code>`.

## Variant

The variants of the language (countries, scripts, standards), what changes between them, and the default.

## Dialogue and punctuation

Quotation marks, dialogue dashes, spacing, ellipses, apostrophes. If you add checks for them to `scripts/check_translation.py`, say here what the script flags.

## Dialogue tags

Whether plain tags are the norm or varied verbs are accepted.

## Forms of address

Familiar and polite forms, politeness levels, pronouns, honorifics, and how to compensate when the source has a distinction the target lacks (or the other way round).

## Names and transliteration

Transcription conventions for foreign names, official forms of real places, and how inflection affects glossary entries.

## Swearing

Where the force of swearing lies in this language, and how to keep the original's force.

## Stock phrases to avoid

The genre clichés translators tend to slip into this language.

## Italics and emphasis

Whether italics are part of the typography, and what replaces them if not.

## Chapter labels

Chapter 3, Prologue, Epilogue in this language.

# Chinese as the target language (`zh`)

> Status: first draft. Native review welcome (see CONTRIBUTING.md).

Read this file when translating **into** Chinese, together with the source-language file. Checker option: `--to zh`.

## Variant

Simplified or traditional characters, chosen by market (mainland China and Singapore; Taiwan, Hong Kong and Macao). It is not only a change of characters: vocabulary (出租车 / 計程車), quotation marks (“…” / 「…」) and the transcription of names change too. Record the choice in the brief.

## Dialogue and punctuation

- “…” (simplified) or 「…」 (traditional), one system for the book.
- Full-width punctuation ，。？！：；; the ellipsis …… and the dash —— doubled; titles of works in 《…》.
- The script flags half-width punctuation between Chinese characters, the two quotation systems mixed, single ellipses and single dashes.

## Language

- **Chengyu** (成语) are the natural equivalent of many idioms, but the compatibility filter and the principle of "same figurative density" apply: don't stuff the text with them, and a literary chengyu in the mouth of a coarse character jars.
- **Time and aspect.** Chinese doesn't conjugate: time is expressed with time words and with 了, 过, 着. Typical translationese: 了 after every verb, passives with 被, repeated pronouns, long chains of attributes with 的.
- **Pronouns.** 他 and 她 distinguish gender only in writing; if the author keeps a gender hidden, avoid the pronoun.
- **Politeness.** 你/您 as familiar/polite. Kinship terms addressed to strangers (大哥, 阿姨) give a foreign character a Chinese color: use them only if the scene supports it.

## Names

Foreign names follow the transcription conventions of the chosen market; real places and people take their established form (基辅 for Kyiv), checked against a source. In the glossary, terms are also found when attached to other words.

## Swearing

Swearing (他妈的, 操, 混蛋…) with the force of the original, as the directive on crude language requires.

## Italics and emphasis

Italics are not part of Chinese typography: emphasis uses 着重号 (dots under the characters) or quotation marks. The script doesn't compare italics.

## Chapter labels

第三章, 序章, 尾声 (尾聲 in traditional characters).

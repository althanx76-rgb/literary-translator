# Russian as the target language (`ru`)

> Status: first draft. Native review welcome (see CONTRIBUTING.md).

Read this file when translating **into** Russian, together with the source-language file. Checker option: `--to ru`.

## When the story is set in Russia, Ukraine or the Soviet Union

The translation takes the characters back to their own language: it is a translation "back home", and the reader knows the story's world better than the author does.

- **Authentic realia.** What the original approximated goes back to its real name: institutions, ranks, offices, acronyms, official names, the bureaucratic and military jargon of the period, songs, food, brands. Check them against sources, not from memory. An error the original's reader doesn't see, a Russian reader sees on the first page: flag it to the author in the notes.
- **Who speaks which language.** In the original everyone speaks the author's language. Characters who in the story speak Ukrainian (or Surzhyk) can keep their lines in Ukrainian or switch to Russian: decide with the author, character by character, and record it in the brief.
- **Politically loaded choices.** Place names (Киев/Київ, Чернобыль/Чорнобиль, Харьков/Харків) and «на Украине» versus «в Украине» are positions, not details: propose them to the author and record them.

## Dialogue and punctuation

- Dialogue with a dash followed by a space: — Иди сюда, — сказал он.
- Quotation marks « », and „ “ inside them.
- The letter ё: always or never, one choice for the book.
- The script flags English or straight quotation marks, a dialogue dash without the following space, and a hyphen or en dash instead of the dash.

## Forms of address

ты / вы. Respect also goes through first name and patronymic (Иван Петрович), intimacy through diminutives (Ваня, Танечка). Record in the brief who calls whom what, and where it changes.

## Swearing

Russian mat has the force of the heaviest swearing in other languages: use it where the original has that force (see the directive on crude language). Since 2014, Russian law requires books containing obscene language to be sold in sealed packaging with a warning: a matter for the publisher, not for the translation.

## Names and transliteration

Foreign names follow Russian transcription conventions (Italian Giovanni → Джованни) and are recorded in the glossary. In the glossary, `*` covers the case endings (Киев* matches Киева, Киеве, Киевом); list a second stem when the stem itself changes.

## Italics and emphasis

Italics are part of Russian typography: the script compares them with the original.

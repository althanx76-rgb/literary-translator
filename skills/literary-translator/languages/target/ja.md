# Japanese as the target language (`ja`)

> Status: first draft. Native review welcome (see CONTRIBUTING.md).

Read this file when translating **into** Japanese, together with the source-language file. Checker option: `--to ja`.

## Register and voice

- For each character choose the politeness level (plain form, です・ます, keigo) according to the relationship with the listener, and the first-person pronoun (私, 僕, 俺, あたし…), which in Japanese defines a character as much as vocabulary does. Record the choices in the brief.
- Narration is normally in the plain form (だ・である).
- **No caricatured role language.** Stereotyped sentence-final particles (わ and かしら for women, じゃ for old men) are used only if the original really characterizes the person that way: they are among the most recognizable marks of dated translations.
- **Subjects and pronouns.** Japanese leaves subjects unsaid more often than European languages do: 彼 and 彼女 repeated in every sentence read as translationese.
- **Honorifics.** For foreign characters in foreign settings, no -さん, -くん, -ちゃん by default: respect goes through register and titles.

## Dialogue and punctuation

- 「…」 for dialogue, 『…』 inside it; titles of works in 『…』.
- Full-width punctuation 、。！？; the ellipsis …… and the dash ―― always doubled.
- The script flags Western quotation marks, half-width punctuation between Japanese characters, single ellipses and unbalanced brackets.

## Names

- Foreign names in katakana, with ・ between first name and surname (マルタ・ロッシ).
- Real places take their current official form: since 2022 the Japanese government has used キーウ and チョルノービリ instead of キエフ and チェルノブイリ. Agree the choice with the author.
- In the glossary, terms are also found when attached to particles.

## Swearing

Swearwords in the strict sense are few: the force lies in brutal register (俺, てめえ, くそ, ちくしょう, ふざけるな) and in insults. Don't dissolve it into politeness.

## Layout, italics and emphasis

- Novels are usually printed vertically (縦書き), with numbers written in kanji in the narration (六時, 三日); the layout is the publisher's decision.
- Italics don't exist in Japanese typography: emphasis uses 傍点 (emphasis dots) or 「」. The script doesn't compare italics.
